About this tool

A browser-based dashboard for processing single-sided NMR relaxometry data from Kea spectrometers: T₂ decays (CPMG), T₁ saturation recovery and T₂–D (SSE-T₂) experiments, with uncertainty estimates, saved figures and tables, and a record of exactly how every result was produced. Build: {{COMMIT}}, {{BUILT}}.

Your data stay on your computer

Everything is computed inside your browser tab. The files you open are read by the page and handed to a Python program running in the browser itself; nothing is uploaded, and the page has no analytics. {{PRIVACY}}

Use at your own risk

  • No warranty, no liability. This software is provided “as is”, without warranty of any kind, and the authors are not liable for any damages arising from its use, as set out in sections 7 and 8 of the Apache License below.
  • Check results independently. Fits, inversions and uncertainty estimates depend on the model, the settings and the noise. They are estimates, not measurements, and the tool can produce a confident-looking answer from data that do not support it. Read the warnings, look at the residuals, and compare with another method before relying on a number.
  • Known limits. The T₂–D inversion is regularised and ill-conditioned: signal outside the range of diffusion coefficients your b-values can resolve is extrapolation, not measurement (the page shades that range). Components that the data cannot determine are flagged but can still appear in a fit.
  • Diffusion weighting. For T₂–D data the b-values are calculated here from the gradient you set (default 23.67 T/m), the storage time Δ and the delays τ in acqu.par, as b = (γG)² τ² (Δ + 2τ/3), not read from the spectrometer; its own axis files are only used as a cross-check. A wrong gradient rescales every diffusion coefficient by the square of the error.
  • Not for clinical, safety-critical or regulatory use. It is research software under development.

Where the methods come from

The inverse-Laplace code was developed by the author, iterated from MATLAB to Python to this tool, following the regularised-inversion approach of Venkataramanan, Song and Hürlimann (IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 50, 1017, 2002). The MCMC uses the emcee sampler (Foreman-Mackey et al., PASP 125, 306, 2013). Full references are in the third-party notices.

Reproducing and citing results

Every save includes provenance.json (a fingerprint of the input files, every setting, the software versions and the build above) and params_used.toml, which the command-line script reads to repeat the analysis. If you publish results from this tool, please give the build shown at the top of this page.
